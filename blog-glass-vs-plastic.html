Glass vs Plastic
Packaging

Beauty & Personal Care · 5 min read

For serum, oil, and skincare brands, the glass-versus-plastic decision usually comes down to more than which looks nicer on a shelf. Here's what actually differs in practice.

Product compatibility

Many actives used in serums and fragrance oils — essential oils, certain acids, concentrated botanical extracts — can degrade some plastics over time, sometimes leaching compounds back into the product or simply breaking down the container itself. Glass is chemically inert, so it doesn't interact with what's inside it. For any formulation with essential oils, high alcohol content, or acidic actives, this alone often settles the decision in glass's favour.

Shelf presence and perceived value

Rightly or wrongly, glass still reads as more premium than plastic at the same price point — the weight in hand, the way light passes through it, the sound it makes. For brands positioning above the mass market, that perception gap matters commercially, not just aesthetically.

Recyclability, realistically

Glass is infinitely recyclable without quality loss, and South Africa has established glass recycling infrastructure through initiatives like The Glass Recycling Company. Plastic recycling, particularly for the smaller cosmetic formats common in beauty packaging, is far less consistently processed — many small plastic containers end up in general waste even when technically recyclable, simply due to sorting economics. If sustainability messaging is part of your brand story, glass is the easier claim to defend.

Where plastic still makes sense

None of this makes plastic universally wrong. It's lighter (lower freight cost), more impact-resistant for products likely to be dropped (bathroom and shower environments), and can be significantly cheaper at high volumes. For travel-size formats or price-sensitive product lines, plastic remains a legitimate choice.

A practical way to decide

  • Formulation contains oils, actives, or high alcohol content: lean glass for compatibility.
  • Product will be used in showers or near water regularly: consider plastic or a glass bottle with protective outer packaging.
  • Brand positioning is premium or sustainability-led: glass supports both claims more credibly.
  • Volumes are high and price sensitivity is the main constraint: plastic may be the more viable starting point, with a move to glass as the brand scales.
